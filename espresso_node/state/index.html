fetch_pruned_leaves 🔒
The leaves at heights, which the data pruned height says are gone, from the query module’s peers through its light client, retrying until they arrive. The light client verifies leaves of every protocol version, and one proof covers the run: it proves the last leaf final and chains the rest to it. The loop needs each leaf once, so the leaves are stored neither in this database, where the pruner would only delete them again, nor in the light client’s cache.
follow_leaves 🔒
Apply every leaf above parent_leaf as it arrives. Leaves come from the local stream, except those the data pruner has already deleted: the availability layer never fetches a leaf at or below the data pruned height, so the stream would wait for one forever. Those come from peers, in runs. Returns when the stream ends, or when a leaf from peers does not extend the parent.
leaf_at 🔒
The leaf at height, from local storage, or from peers once the data pruner has deleted it.
pruned_past 🔒
The data pruned height when it is at or above height. The leaf and header there are out of local reach: the pruner deleted them, and the availability layer never fetches below its marker. A failed read counts as not pruned; every caller asks again before long, and the loop must not end over a transient database error.
